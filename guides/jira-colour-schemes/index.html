Jira board colour schemes: 5 ready-made sets

Short answer: a good Jira board colour scheme uses a few colours, each with one meaning: red for blocked or urgent work, a calm colour per status, a stripe for softer signals like "due this week", and a limit on the busy columns. Below are 5 ready-made sets for Jira Cloud boards, with label, status, column and card colours. Each one can be applied in one click in Tintboard for Jira, or downloaded and imported.

Jira Cloud can't colour labels, choose a colour per status or colour cards on team-managed boards. These schemes need Tintboard for Jira (free for up to 10 users), which adds all of that for every board on the site.

How to use one

  • In the app: open Settings → Apps → Tintboard → Overview, pick the scheme under Start from a ready-made scheme, adjust anything, and save.
  • From a file: download it below and open it in a text editor. In Settings → Apps → Tintboard → Backup, first click Export to the box and keep a copy of what's there, then paste the scheme into the box, click Import from the box, check the result and save. Importing replaces all your Tintboard settings, fields on cards and per-project colours included, so if you use those, the in-app way above is better: it only replaces the label, status, column and card colours. Nothing changes for your team until you save.

Label and status names must match yours: rename them in the app before saving if your workflow uses other words. Every colour keeps readable text (WCAG AA). To check how your own colours look to colour-blind teammates, try the free Jira colour checker.

  • Untouched for a week: a stripe on the left when not updated for 7 days
  • My cards: a stripe on the left when assigned to the person looking

Make your own

Start from the scheme closest to your board and change it: Tintboard previews every colour on a Jira-look card, column and backlog row before you save. Keep it small: three to five label colours, one colour per status category, and card rules for what the team must act on.
